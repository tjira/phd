\chapter{\foldertotitle}\label{chp:mixed_quantum_classical_dynamics}
Even with the efficient numerical schemes from Chapter~\ref{chp:time_evolution}, solving the \acrshort{tdse} for large systems is still computationally infeasible. Under normal conditions, the \acrshort{boa} allows us to separate nuclear and electronic motion. However, when two or more electronic states approach degeneracy, this separation fails and nonadiabatic effects become highly significant. To overcome this challenge, we must rely on mixed quantum-classical dynamics.

This chapter details \acrfull{tsh} as the primary framework to bridge this gap. By propagating nuclei classically on a single adiabatic potential energy surface while treating electrons quantum mechanically, \acrshort{tsh} captures essential nonadiabatic quantum effects at a fraction of the computational cost.

the theoretical frameworks detailed here form the basis of the practical algorithms implemented in the custom \textsc{Zinq} software developed for this thesis.\supercite{10.5281/zenodo.18386143} We provide a rigorous description of \acrfull{fssh} and \acrfull{lzsh}, which are the foundational methods deployed in our primary research on curvature-driven algorithms (Sec.~\ref{sec:curvature_driven_surface_hopping_algorithms}) and photodynamics sensitivity analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity_analysis_in_photodynamics}). Furthermore, we discuss the \acrfull{mash}, which is heavily leveraged to analyze wavefunction coherence in our supporting numerical studies (Sec.~\ref{sec:coherence_in_mixed_qc_dynamics}).
