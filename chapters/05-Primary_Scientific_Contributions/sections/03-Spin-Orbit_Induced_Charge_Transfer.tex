\section{\filetotitle}
\textit{Commentary on: \fullcite{10.1016/j.jqsrt.2025.109697}}

The next published work of this thesis focuses on spin-orbit induced charge transfer from article \textit{Spin--Orbit Induced Charge Transfer in \ce{H^+}/\ce{D^+}/\ce{T^+}-\ce{N(^4S)} Collisions} in \textit{Journal of Quantitative Spectroscopy and Radiative Transfer}.

The study focuses on the investigation of charge transfer processes in collisions between protons (\ce{H^+}), deuterons (\ce{D^+}), tritons (\ce{T^+}) and nitrogen atoms in their ground state (\ce{N}($^4$S)). The charge transfer in such collisions can be induced by spin-orbit coupling, rotational couplings, and radial nonadiabatic couplings. The first study of these collisions were performed more than 30 years ago using approximate potentials and Landau--Zener theory. Since then, no further studies have been conducted on this system.

I context of nonadiabatic dynamics simulations, the accurate description of \acrfull{soc} is often challenging, expecially when combined with the need to cover a wide range of collision energies. The study presented in this article aims to address this challenge by employing a combination of high-level quantum-mechanical calculations, Landau--Zener theory, and nonadiabatic dynamics simulations to investigate the charge transfer processes in the \ce{N(^4S) + H^+ -> N^+(^3P) + H(^2S)} reaction.

To calculate the cross sections for the charge transfer process, the study first computes the \acrshort{pes} for the relevant electronic states ($^4\Sigma$ and $^2\Pi$) of the \ce{N-H^+}, \ce{N-D^+} and \ce{N-T^+} systems using MRCI/aug-cc-pVQZ-F12 level of theory. The \acrshort{soc} matrix elements are also calculated at the same level of theory. The \acrshort{pes} and \acrshort{soc} matrix elements are then used to perform 1D quantum dynamics simulations (as described in Sec~\ref{sec:split_operator_method}) to obtain the charge transfer probabilities $P(E)$ as a function of collision energy $E$. Note that even though the impact factor dependence is not explicitly included in the 1D quantum dynamics simulations, the energy $E$ and impact factor $b$ are in Landau--Zener theory related via the crossing point $r_x$ and the activation energy $E_x$ and the charge transfer probabilities can be reduced to
\begin{equation}
P(E,b)=P\left(E-\frac{b^2E}{r_x^2}\right).
\end{equation}
This allows to calculate the cross sections $\sigma(E)$ from the 1D quantum dynamics simulations. The results from 2D quantum dynamics simulations are included in the supporint information as they are not the main subject of this article.

The results from the quantum dynamics simulations are compared to the predictions from Landau--Zener theory, which provides an analytical expression for the charge transfer probabilities based on the \acrshort{soc} matrix elements and the slopes of the \acrshort{pes} at the crossing points. Using the charge transfer probabilities, the study calculates the cross sections for the charge transfer process using
\begin{equation}
\sigma(E)=2\pi\int_0^{(1-\frac{E_x}{E})r_x}bP(E,b)\symrm{d}b,
\end{equation}
where $E_x$ is the activation energy for the charge transfer process and $r_x$ is the crossing point between the relevant \acrshort{pes}.

The calculated cross sections from 1D quantum dynamics simulations and Landau--Zener theory are presented in Fig~\ref{fig:soc_charge_transfer_cross_sections}. For brevity, only the results for the \ce{N-D^+} and \ce{N-T^+} system are shown here, the \ce{N-H^+} results can be found in the article. The results show that both methods yield similar cross sections which confirm the validity of Landau--Zener theory in describing the charge transfer process in this system.

Additionaly, rate constants for the charge transfer process are calculated by integrating the cross sections over a Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution of collision energies as
\begin{equation}
k(T)=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi\mu(k_BT)^3}}\int_0^\infty E\sigma(E)\e^{-\frac{E}{k_BT}}\symrm{d}E,
\end{equation}
where $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant, $T$ is the temperature and $\mu$ is the reduced mass of the system. The calculated rate constants are presented in Fig~\ref{fig:soc_rate_constants} as a function of temperature.

Overall, the study presented in this article provides a comprehensive investigation of spin-orbit induced charge transfer processes in \ce{H^+/D^+/T^+}-\ce{N(^4S)} collisions. The combination of high-level quantum-mechanical calculations, Landau--Zener theory, and nonadiabatic dynamics simulations allows for a detailed understanding of the charge transfer mechanisms and the calculation of accurate cross sections and rate constants.
