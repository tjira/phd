\chapter{\foldertotitle}\label{chp:time_evolution}
Quantum dynamics describes the evolution of quantum states $\ket{\Psi(t)}\in\mathcal{H}$ over time and is fundamental in quantum chemistry for understanding molecular behavior at the atomic level. It is governed by the principles of quantum mechanics, particularly the \acrfull{tdse}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tdse}
\i\ddt\ket{\Psi\left(t\right)}=H\ket{\Psi\left(t\right)},
\end{equation}
which dictates how wavefunctions change with time. Due to the complex nature of these equations, practical solutions are often limited to small or model systems.

Because of this bottleneck, we must rely on robust numerical schemes to make time evolution computationally viable. This chapter focuses on evolution of quantum states over time, a fundamental aspect of quantum mechanics and the main focus of this thesis. The motivation for this chapter is to introduce the numerical schemes for solving the \acrshort{tdse} that make simulating the time evolution computationally viable. Specifically, the Split-Operator method described here serves as the core numerical engine for the custom \textsc{Zinq} code developed as part of this thesis.\supercite{10.5281/zenodo.18386143} Understanding this exact quantum dynamics is crucial, as it serves as the gold standard benchmark for the mixed quantum-classical methods evaluated in Chapter~\ref{chp:mixed_quantum_classical_dynamics}. The methods described in this chapter are also used to calculate the exact charge transfer cross-sections in Sec.~\ref{sec:spin_orbit_induced_charge_transfer}.

To ensure these time-evolution operators are mathematically rigorous, we work within the complex separable Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ introduced in Sec.~\ref{sec:hilbert_spaces_and_quantum_states}, with self-adjoint Hamiltonian $H$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:self_adjoint_operators_and_observables}), so that the time-evolution operators described in this chapter are consistent with functional calculus treatment in Sec.~\ref{sec:spectral_theory_in_hilbert_spaces}.
