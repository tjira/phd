\part{Theoretical Foundations}
This part of the dissertation describes the theoretical foundations of the work. It is intended to provide a detailed overview of the mathematical and physical concepts that are relevant to the research presented in the second part of the dissertation. All of the methods and techniques described in this part were implemented by the author in the custom software package \textsc{Zinq}.\supercite{10.5281/zenodo.18386143} The part is organized into three chapters, each of which focuses on a specific aspect of nonadiabatic dynamics.

The first chapter provides a comprehensive overview of the necessary mathematical background. It starts with a review of linear algebra, including vector spaces, linear transformations, and eigenvalue problems. It then introduces the concept of Hilbert spaces and the mathematical framework of quantum mechanics like \acrfull{boa} and the Born rule.

The second chapter delves deeply into the time evolution of quantum systems. It is focused on the methods for solving the \acrfull{tdse}, including the some of the numerically exact as well as approximate methods. The chapter also contains a section with fully derived \glspl{eom} for some of the popular parametric wavefunction ansatzes used in variational quantum dynamics that are rarely found in the literature.

The last chapter of this part is dedicated to the theory of mixed quantum-classical dynamics. It is mostly focused on the \acrfull{tsh} method, which is a widely used approach for simulating nonadiabatic dynamics. The chapter also discusses the limitations of these methods as well as various approaches to overcome these limitations.
