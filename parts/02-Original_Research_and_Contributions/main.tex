\part{Original Research and Contributions}
Contrary to the previous parts of this thesis, which were mostly focused on reviewing the state of the art algorithms and methods in the field of nonadiabatic dynamics, this part of the thesis is focused on the original research and contributions of the author. The research presented in the following chapters covers a wide range of topics, from the development of methods for nonadiabatic dynamics, to the application of these methods to a variety of systems, incuding model systems, atomic collisions, and molecular systems. The research presented in this part contains selected projects that the author has been involved in during his doctoral studies, and is not meant to be an exhaustive list of all the research that the author has been involved in. The research presented in this part is organized in two chapters.

The first chapter contains results that never made it to a publication, but are still important for the understanding of how the methods work and how they can be improved. These results are more focused on the foundations of the methods, and on the numerical studies that were performed to understand the behavior of the methods. These results are important for the understanding of how the methods work, and for the development of new methods in the future.

The second chapter contains work that has been published in peer-reviewed journals, and that represents the primary scientific contributions of the author. These contributions are more focused on the application of the previously mentioned methods to a variety of systems, including model systems, atomic collisions, and molecular systems.
