\chapter{Conclusion and Outlook}
Theoretical chemistry is defined by the constant tension between accuracy and computational cost. This thesis aimed to bridge the divergence between numerically exact quantum dynamics and approximate mixed quantum-classical methods, directly addressing the limitations imposed by electronic structure and coupling approximations. A foundational step in overcoming this gap was the development of the \textsc{Zinq} software package, which serves as a unified platform to implement, validate, and compare these diverse dynamical approaches. Using this toolkit, I have been, for example, able assess the reliability of curvature-driven surface hopping algorithms (Chapter~\ref{sec:curvature_driven_surface_hopping_algorithms}) and calculate scattering cross sections for ion-atom collisions in the presence of strong \gls{soc} (Chapter~\ref{sec:spin_orbit_induced_charge_transfer}).

With this toolkit, I have pushed the boundaries of surface hopping methods to evaluate their limits and identify areas for improvement. My exploration of curvature-driven surface hopping algorithms demonstrated the fundamental limits of such theoretical shortcuts. Bypassing the need for \glspl{nacv}, these methods can be computationally efficient, but they may not always capture the full complexity of nonadiabatic processes. My work has highlighted the importance of carefully assessing the applicability of these methods by comparing them against more traditional approaches like \gls{fssh}.

While algorithmic rigor in quantum dynamics is crucial, my sensitivity analysis of \textit{cis}-stilbene photodynamics has revealed that the choice of the electronic structure method is equally important. I showed that relying solely on short-time observables, such as \gls{trpes}, is insufficient to correctly validate the method's ability to predict long-time photoproduct quantum yields. Because even minor energetic shifts in \gls{pes} can lead to vastly different photoproduct distributions, this work serves as a cautionary tale for the field, emphasizing the need for comprehensive validation of electronic structure methods in the context of nonadiabatic dynamics.

Beyond molecular photochemistry, this work extends the application of quantum dynamics to collision physics. By applying exact wavepacket propagation methods to the study of ion-atom collisions, I investigated the spin--orbit induced charge transfer processes governing these interactions. This application provides a direct comparison between exact quantum dynamics and analytical transition theories in the presence of strong \gls{soc}, offering insights into the underlying physical processes of ion-atom interactions.

This work also highlights the persistent limitations in the field. The exact dynamics as implemented in \textsc{Zinq}, while highly efficient, still faces the exponential scaling problem, which limits its applicability to small systems. Moreover, the electronic structure remains the largest source of uncertainty in nonadiabatic dynamics simulations, meaning validation on specific systems does not guarantee general applicability. Looking forward, the limits of \gls{tsh} without explicit \glspl{nacv} remain an open question. Addressing these challenges through the continued refinement of the \textsc{Zinq} software will be the focus of my future work.
