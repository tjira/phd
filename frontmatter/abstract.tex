\chapter*{Abstract}
The simulations of nonadiabatic dynamics are crucial for understanding various chemical processes, such as photochemical reactions and energy transfer. This thesis presents a comprehensive study of computational strategies for simulating nonadiabatic dynamics, with a focus on the development and application of novel algorithms. I explore the efficiency and accuracy of different methods, including various numerically exact approaches and approximate methods contained under the collective name of mixed quantum-classical dynamics.

Comprehensive derivations of all the discussed methods are provided to ensure a clear understanding of the underlying mathematical principles and physical assumptions. The performance of these techniques is evaluated through a series of benchmarks, with a strong emphasis on their practical computational implementation. Building on these theoretical foundations, I apply these methods to a range of model systems and real molecular systems to investigate their nonadiabatic behavior. This includes a critical evaluation of the extreme sensitivity of \gls{tsh} to underlying electronic structure methods, as well as an assessment of approximations that attempt to bypass the need for explicitly calculated \glspl{nacv}. Furthermore, the scope is broadened to atomic collision dynamics governed by strong \gls{soc}, where multidimensional wavepacket propagation and analytical transition theories are integrated to model complex charge transfer events.

The findings of this thesis contribute to the advancement of computational techniques for simulating nonadiabatic dynamics, offering valuable tools and physical insights for researchers in the fields of theoretical chemistry and chemical physics.
