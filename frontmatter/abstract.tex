\chapter*{Abstract}
Simulations of nonadiabatic dynamics are crucial for understanding various chemical processes, such as photochemical reactions and energy transfer. This thesis presents a study of computational strategies for simulating nonadiabatic dynamics, with a focus on the development and application of novel algorithms. I explore the efficiency and accuracy of different methods, including various numerically exact approaches and approximate methods collectively referred to as mixed quantum-classical dynamics.

Comprehensive derivations of all discussed methods are provided to ensure a clear understanding of the underlying mathematical principles and physical assumptions. The performance of these techniques is evaluated through a series of benchmarks, with a strong emphasis on their practical computational implementation within a custom software framework. Building on these theoretical foundations, I apply these methods to a range of model systems and real molecular systems to investigate their nonadiabatic behavior. This includes a critical evaluation of the strong sensitivity of \gls{tsh} to underlying electronic structure methods, as well as an assessment of approximations that attempt to bypass the need for explicitly calculated \glspl{nacv}. Furthermore, the scope is broadened to atomic collision dynamics governed by strong \gls{soc}, where multidimensional wavepacket propagation and analytical transition theories are employed to model complex charge transfer events.
